# VARDHAN Q-CORE — Gated Commercial Path to £575K

**Purpose:** turn the £15K discovery → £60K proof of value → £500K production sequence into a buyer-evidence-led sales process.

## Commercial principle

£575,000 is the sum of three potential contracts, not booked revenue, a forecast, ARR, or a buyer commitment. Count each stage as contracted only when its agreement is executed; count it as collected only when payment clears. Do not describe £500K as annual recurring revenue unless the signed commercial structure makes it recurring.

The product proposition to test with buyers is narrow: VARDHAN Q-CORE is intended to help enterprise systems validate evidence provenance, reject tampered or replayed submissions, and produce independently verifiable trust receipts. Whether this solves a material buyer problem remains a discovery hypothesis, not an established customer outcome.

## Stage gates

| Stage | Value | Entry evidence | Exit evidence required before advancing |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Discovery | £15,000 | A relevant buyer acknowledges the problem question; a plausible use case and sponsor emerge | Signed scope and terms; agreed deliverables; problem confirmed as worthwhile; feasibility/access assessed; PoV acceptance criteria agreed |
| Proof of value | £60,000 | Discovery accepted; buyer agrees the problem is worth solving; technical feasibility and access confirmed | Pre-agreed tests and business metrics executed; baseline comparison; security findings disclosed; sponsor accepts results; production requirements documented |
| Production | £500,000 | PoV passes agreed acceptance criteria and sponsor supports production | Signed production contract/order; commercial basis and term confirmed; deployment/security scope, support/SLA, procurement, and acceptance agreed |

**No automatic progression.** Discovery can recommend stop; a PoV can be failed or inconclusive. Those are valid evidence-led outcomes.

## Stage 1 — £15K discovery

See [Discovery scope](DISCOVERY_15K_SCOPE.md). Sell an actionable decision, not a claim that the platform is production-ready. Deliver a current-state map, prioritized risks, architecture/integration options, readiness gaps, and a scoped PoV plan.

## Stage 2 — £60K proof of value

See [PoV scope](POV_60K_SCOPE.md). Prove one workflow in a bounded environment. Agree the baseline, data, workload, success thresholds, security boundary, and test owners before execution. Do not promise a production SLA, certification, or broad rollout as part of the PoV.

## Stage 3 — £500K production

See [Production scope](PRODUCTION_500K_SCOPE.md). £500K is a planning value until buyer and seller define whether the agreement is an implementation fee, license/support term, recurring subscription, or combination. The contract must specify environments, integrations, tenants/nodes, support, service levels, operational responsibilities, and acceptance.

## Immediate execution sequence

1. **Pick one target account and one buyer problem.** Use public evidence to form a question; do not infer internal architecture, incidents, budget, or intent.
2. **Send a problem question, not a £15K proposal.** Use [first-buyer outreach](FIRST_BUYER_OUTREACH.md). The immediate objective is a response from a relevant buyer and a conversation to validate the problem.
3. **Qualify the buying path.** Identify sponsor, technical owner, security/procurement route, access constraints, timing, budget path, and decision process. Keep unknowns marked UNKNOWN.
4. **Offer discovery only when relevant.** Send the £15K scope and terms for acceptance. An unacknowledged lead or unaccepted proposal is not contracted.
5. **End discovery with a signed PoV plan or a stop decision.** Do not start the £60K scope before access, feasibility, and acceptance criteria are agreed.
6. **Convert a successful PoV into a production decision pack.** Map results to deployment, security remediation, support, procurement, and commercial terms.
7. **Track facts separately:** target → buyer acknowledged → qualified → proposal sent → signed → delivery accepted → invoiced → paid. Pipeline is not cash.

## Buyer qualification record

For every target, record organization and source-backed relevance; named buyer and role; exact question and dated response; buyer-stated impact/current workaround; sponsor and technical evaluator; security/procurement/legal route; available data/environment/access; discovery proposal/terms status; PoV feasibility and metrics; production deployment, integrations, security, support/SLA, recovery, residency and audit requirements; commercial basis/term/payment/VAT/expenses; stage, next action, owner, and evidence link.

Unknown fields remain UNKNOWN; do not replace missing buyer evidence with assumptions.

## Commercial and technical guardrails

- Do not claim production readiness, independent certification, a completed penetration test, or customer-proven ROI without matching evidence.
- Link every technical PASS claim to a specific test run, report, or reproducible artifact.
- Distinguish lab/demo validation from live customer-controlled integration.
- Use the [enterprise completion matrix](../ENTERPRISE_COMPLETION_MATRIX.md) and [release gate report](../../RELEASE_GATE_REPORT.md) as technical readiness sources. Unchecked gates and documented limitations must be reflected in proposals.
- Include explicit out-of-scope sections in discovery and PoV offers.
- State VAT, expenses, payment milestones, and acceptance terms in the actual offer. This planning document is not a contract or legal advice.
- Do not describe £575K as committed revenue until contracts are signed; do not call it ARR without a recurring contract basis.
