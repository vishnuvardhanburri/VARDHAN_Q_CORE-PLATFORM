# LGT — Buyer Hypothesis and First-Contact Brief

**Status:** target-account hypothesis only. This document does not establish that LGT has a confirmed security gap, active PQC procurement, budget, intent to buy, or an agreed project.

**Prepared:** 15 October 2026

## Account hypothesis

LGT is an existing outreach target in the Q-CORE evidence-pack history. The first relevant question to test is whether a financial-services team needs better independent verification and audit evidence across cryptographic/security changes, findings, and approvals. The commercial use case must come from the buyer—not from our assumption.

Before outreach, confirm the exact contracting/customer entity and geography (LGT Group versus a specific bank or operating company), the recipient's current role, and why that person is a relevant owner.

## Public context: what is and is not established

1. **LGT's public report portal exists.** LGT's official page lists the LGT Group Annual Report 2025 and Disclosure Report 2025, among other reports. This provides a legitimate starting point for account research; it does not demonstrate an LGT PQC initiative or a Q-CORE need. Source: https://www.lgt.com/global-en/publications/reports
2. **Financial-sector PQC migration is a public industry topic.** The G7 Cyber Expert Group's statement published by HM Treasury on 13 January 2026 discusses cryptographic agility, inventory/discovery, risk planning, testing, and validation. The statement explicitly says it is contextual and does not set regulatory expectations. It is not evidence of LGT's intent or obligations. Source: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/advancing-a-coordinated-roadmap-for-the-transition-to-post-quantum-cryptography-in-the-financial-sector/g7-cyber-expert-group-statement-on-advancing-a-coordinated-roadmap-for-the-transition-to-post-quantum-cryptography-in-the-financial-sector-january-20
3. **The NCSC publishes PQC migration timelines for large organizations.** Its guidance includes discovery and planning milestones and notes that timelines should inform preparation. Applicability to a specific LGT entity, jurisdiction, or control environment must not be assumed. Source: https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/pqc-migration-timelines

## Falsifiable problem hypothesis

A financial-services security, cryptography, technology-risk, or architecture team may need to demonstrate that a security finding or cryptographic-migration decision is bound to the correct actor, organization, policy/version, and evidence; that tampering or replay is rejected; and that reviewers can independently verify the resulting audit evidence.

**What we do not know:** whether LGT has this problem, how it handles it today, how severe or costly it is, who owns it, whether an initiative is active, which controls it requires, or whether budget/procurement exists. Ask rather than infer.

## First-contact roles to validate

Prioritize the role closest to the confirmed problem, not seniority alone:

- CISO or delegated security engineering/architecture sponsor.
- Head of security architecture, cryptography, or PQC migration (if such a role exists).
- Technology risk, security assurance, or cryptographic governance owner.

Do not invent a person's name/title or use an unverified contact. Confirm current role and an appropriate business contact channel before sending.

## Suggested first message

**Subject:** Question on evidence assurance for cryptographic change

Hi [Name],

I’m reaching out with one question rather than assume LGT has a specific gap.

Financial-sector guidance is increasingly discussing cryptographic discovery, migration planning, and validation. In programs involving multiple teams and systems, has maintaining independently verifiable evidence of security findings, cryptographic changes, and approvals become a problem your team is trying to solve—or is this not a current priority at LGT?

We are developing VARDHAN Q-CORE, a trust layer intended to bind evidence to identity and policy and reject tampered or replayed submissions. It has controlled-lab evidence, but it has not been independently security-assessed and we are not presenting it as production-ready. We are first trying to determine whether the use case is relevant and what a buyer would require as proof.

Would a short conversation be useful to understand your current process and success criteria?

Regards,  
[Sender]

## Qualification questions if they reply

1. Is the problem active now, and what workflow exposes it?
2. What is the consequence or cost of today's approach?
3. Who owns the outcome and who can sponsor a paid discovery?
4. What evidence/data/environment can be shared under the team's security rules?
5. What would make a bounded proof of value successful, which metrics would be used, and who approves them?
6. What security review and procurement route would a discovery or PoV need?

## Gate to a £15K discovery

Do not send a formal price proposal based on a generic response alone. Move to a discovery proposal only when a relevant buyer acknowledges a real question and agrees to discuss scope. The signed discovery SOW must define deliverables, buyer responsibilities, access/data rules, exclusions, acceptance procedure, fee, VAT/payment terms, and schedule.

Record all buying-process fields as UNKNOWN until the buyer supplies evidence. A no, non-response, or lack of sponsor is a qualification result—not a reason to inflate pipeline.
