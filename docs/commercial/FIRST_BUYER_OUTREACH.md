# First Buyer Outreach — Problem Validation

**Objective:** obtain a response from a relevant buyer on whether the problem is real and timely. This is not a £15K pitch or a claim that Q-CORE is production-ready.

## Before sending

Fill the placeholders only after verifying the recipient's role and a source-backed reason the question is relevant. Do not claim a prospect has a particular vulnerability, architecture, incident, quantum-migration program, budget, or procurement timeline unless the prospect has stated it or a reliable public source supports it.

## Email template

**Subject:** Question about evidence integrity and verification

Hi [Name],

I’m working on VARDHAN Q-CORE, a trust layer intended to help systems validate evidence provenance, reject tampered or replayed submissions, and produce independently verifiable receipts.

I’m trying to establish whether this is a live problem for [team/use case]—particularly where findings or approvals cross system boundaries and need to be independently checked.

Is that a problem your team is actively trying to solve, or is it not a current priority?

If it is relevant, would a short conversation be useful to see whether a bounded architecture and security discovery would be worthwhile? I would first want to understand your workflow and acceptance requirements rather than assume a fit.

Regards,  
[Sender]

## What counts as progress

- **Acknowledged:** a relevant buyer replies to the question.
- **Qualified:** buyer confirms a relevant problem, why it matters, and an identifiable sponsor/next step.
- **Discovery opportunity:** buyer agrees to discuss scope, deliverables, access/dependencies, and terms for a £15K discovery.
- **Contracted:** a valid discovery agreement is executed.
- **Paid:** invoice is collected.

A sent email, open, connection, generic “interesting,” or internal enthusiasm is not a contracted stage.

## Follow-up conversation: five questions

1. Which exact workflow creates the evidence-integrity or verification problem?
2. What goes wrong today, and what is the impact or cost of the current workaround?
3. Who owns the risk, and who can sponsor a bounded evaluation?
4. What systems/data/environment could be made available, under which security rules?
5. What must the buyer observe to approve a £60K PoV, and who signs off?

Do not jump to a price before establishing relevance. If the problem is not current or no sponsor exists, record that and stop or revisit later.
